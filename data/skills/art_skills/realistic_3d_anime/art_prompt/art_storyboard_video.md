# 视频画风与动作 · 电影级半写实三维

## Video storytelling rules

Generate cinematic visual descriptions. Prioritize character consistency, physical motion, camera movement, then environment. Preserve the Shot, Camera and Action information of the supplied storyboard without imposing a fixed H3 section structure.

Reference characters retain the same face, facial proportions, hairstyle, hair length, body proportions, costume structure and accessories in every frame. The complete character sheet occupies one Picture and defines one Subject; its panels are different views of the same person, not separate uploads. The environment reference establishes architecture, background, spatial layout, lighting and atmosphere. Asset drawing requests do not override the selected images.

The video's rendering follows the currently selected images. Apply video_prefix.md by reading their visible degree of realism, eye-to-face size, eyelid shape, nose structure, jaw contour and skin, hair and fabric surface appearance; do not assign a new genre from the project preset. Establish a few observed qualities in one or two English opening sentences and include them in the character's preserved reference role. Scene lighting and required wetness adapt those existing surfaces without changing the character design or inventing unresolved skin detail. A close-up or strong emotion uses the same facial geometry as the reference. Characters and environment share coherent lighting and three-dimensional treatment.

## Action

The storyboard determines the initial pose and support, while a character sheet determines identity and rendering. State the actor's body position, hand contacts, weight-bearing support and foot placement before describing motion. Start directly in the requested state and carry it across cuts. If the actor is already hanging by the hands, show hands bearing the weight above the head, the torso and both legs below them, and both feet dangling in open space outside the edge. A reference image of the actor standing with a railing is not a pose instruction. Apply this support/contact reasoning only where the story requires it; do not invent a fall, landing or rescue.

Describe visible results with short, concrete action sentences. Prefer “The character loses balance near the railing” when loss of balance is the supplied event. If the story explicitly includes a push, write “A pushes B. B loses balance at the railing” rather than a long nested chain; do not erase the actor, reverse roles or change a deliberate act into an accident. Keep one principal action beat at a time. Do not add a clothing tear, secondary injury, extra gesture or new plot event to fill a word count.

Avoid overly detailed static character inventories, excessive artistic adjectives and long causal action chains. Use physical contact, weight transfer and a clear before/after state to make the action readable. Hair, fabric and environmental motion support the main action rather than compete for attention.

Locate the contact within a connected scene. A railing must attach to the deck edge through visible supports. For a person hanging outside a ship, show the local ship side beside the body and open water directly below the feet; the deck ends behind the railing, with no deck under the person. Frame that local edge instead of placing the person in front of the reference's whole-ship view. This spatial relation belongs in the shot description, not only in the reference analysis.

## Camera

Translate character 全景 as a full-body/full shot that includes the feet and the relevant contact/support points. Reserve an environment-dominated wide establishing shot for 远景/大远景 or an explicitly requested location introduction. The first frame must already show the requested physical state; do not open on an empty ship when the shot requires a hanging person. Keep one feasible camera path and the original cut timing. Reconcile a camera direction with the established body geometry: moving from overhead gripping hands to the face is downward. Do not alter the story action to make an incompatible camera word fit. Subsequent close-ups retain the same body/support state even when the feet are outside the frame.

## Sound and continuity

Keep the supplied dialogue complete, preserve its speaker and meaning, and synchronize mouth movement with the selected language. Use concise physical sounds and continuous ambience; do not add speech or music without a story requirement. Preserve identity and clothing through motion and occlusion. Explicit transformations change only the stated attributes at the supplied story boundary.

Keep exact Subject/Picture mapping inside the official six-section Ref2VA structure. Spend detail on the visible scene, not repeated static descriptions; do not expand a simple event into multiple actions only to reach a word target.
